# The scam catalog

Each entry: **the setup** (how it starts) → **the tell** (what gives it away
before it costs anything) → **the counter**.

Naming the playbook to a stressed traveler is half the help. "This is the
mid-route renegotiation — it ends the moment you and your bag are out of the
car" tells them how it finishes, which panic does not.

---

## Universal tells

These generalize better than any individual scam. If two or more are present,
it's a setup regardless of whether it matches anything below.

- **They approached you.** Nearly every scam in this file starts with a stranger
  initiating contact. Genuine vendors wait behind their goods.
- **Manufactured urgency.** "Last day", "closes in ten minutes", "the tour leaves
  now", "only today, festival price."
- **The price appears after you're committed** — in the moving car, seated at the
  table, holding the item, wearing the bracelet, on the camel.
- **You're being moved.** "It's better over there", "my cousin's shop", "the real
  entrance is this way." Every meter away from a public place favors them.
- **You can't leave freely.** The single most reliable indicator that this stopped
  being a transaction.
- **Unusual, unsolicited helpfulness** from someone with no reason to help, who
  then does not want to be thanked and leave.
- **They know something they shouldn't** — your name, your hotel, your flight.
  Means they read your luggage tag or your booking, not that they're official.
- **The number changed** between what you heard, what was written, and what
  you're being billed.
- **They refuse a paper trail.** No receipt, no itemized bill, no meter, no app.

## Transport

**"Meter's broken" / no meter.**
Setup: driver waves off the meter, names a price later or "we'll see at the end."
Tell: an unused meter that works for locals. Counter: agree a number before the
door closes, or don't get in. If already moving, say the number you'll pay now
and repeat it; don't wait for the end.

**The mid-route renegotiation.** *(Egypt, Morocco, India, Vietnam)*
Setup: the car stops somewhere unremarkable and the driver announces the agreed
price is no longer the price — traffic, wrong info, luggage, "you said the other
Zamalek." Tell: the stop is in a place with no reason to stop. Counter: it ends
when you're out with your bag, not when you win the argument. Get the luggage
out of the trunk *first*, then pay the agreed amount, exact change, and walk. In
a safe public spot, name the alternative ("Uber is 90") and hold. On an empty
road at night — Rule 0, pay and go.

**"Your hotel is closed / burned down / overbooked."**
Setup: driver takes a call, or just announces it, and offers a better place.
Tell: he knows a competitor's availability. Counter: "Drive to the address. I'll
check myself." Call the hotel on speaker in front of him. Never let the
destination change while you're in the car.

**Long-hauling.**
Setup: a 15-minute trip takes 45. Tell: the map on your own phone. Counter: keep
the route open and visible from the start — that alone prevents it. Afterwards,
ride-hail apps refund route deviations readily.

**Invented surcharges on arrival.**
Setup: at the destination, add luggage, night, airport, bridge, tunnel,
per-person, air-conditioning fees. Tell: they show up only after the ride.
Counter: "The price was X" and pay X. Surcharges that are real are posted and
apply to locals too.

**The ride-hail driver who wants cash instead.** *(Cairo above all, also Delhi,
Lagos, Hanoi)*
Setup: the driver accepts the booking, then calls or messages demanding more than
the app fare — before pickup, on arrival, or once the luggage is loaded. The
escalation is "cancel it in the app, I'll still take you, cash." Tell: any request
to move money off the platform. Counter: before pickup, cancel it yourself and
pick the reason "driver requested payment outside the app" — it's a named option
and it waives the cancellation fee — then rebook, and expect to cycle through
several drivers. Once you're already riding, do **not** cancel: a cancelled trip
has no GPS trace, no fare record, no dispute route, and no insurance, which turns
a documented ride into an unrecorded car with a stranger. Ride it out and pay the
app fare only. Have a second app installed (Careem in Egypt and the Gulf, Bolt in
Turkey and Africa, Grab/Gojek in SE Asia) — switching apps beats arguing.

**Airport interception.**
Setup: a man inside arrivals with a phone greets you by name, or says he's your
ride-hail driver, or works the "official taxi desk" that isn't. Tell: he found
you before you found him. Counter: match the plate and the driver's name in the
app, every time. Walk out to the marked rank or the app pickup zone. Never
follow anyone to a car park.

**The commission tour.** *(Delhi, Bangkok, Cairo, Marrakech)*
Setup: absurdly cheap tuk-tuk/taxi "city tour, 10 rupees / 20 baht, one hour."
Tell: the price is below the cost of fuel. Counter: you're the delivery vehicle
to three shops that pay him. Fine if you want the ride and can say no in the
shops; otherwise decline.

**"The ticket office is closed."** *(train and bus stations, India, Egypt, Turkey)*
Setup: a helpful man at the station entrance says the counter is closed / on
strike / moved, and leads you to a "government tourist office" that's a travel
agency. Tell: the counter is never closed. Counter: walk to the actual counter
and look with your own eyes. Buy in the official app where one exists.

## Shopping

**The export/investment goods scam.** *(Bangkok gems, Istanbul & Kashmir carpets,
Chinese "tea ceremony")*
Setup: a friendly stranger, a chain of coincidences, and a chance to buy
something you can resell at home for many times the price. Tell: an unsolicited
introduction to a business opportunity. Counter: there is no arbitrage available
to a tourist. Losses here run into thousands and are essentially unrecoverable.
The scam is old, well-documented, and still works because it's slow and social.

**The factory stop.** *(Egyptian papyrus and perfume oils, Moroccan argan and
tanneries, Indian handicraft emporiums)*
Setup: a "workshop demonstration" en route to somewhere you wanted to go. Tell:
it wasn't on your itinerary. Counter: fine to look; the demonstration is a
selling floor with a closing pitch. Say up front that you're not buying today.

**Unit switch.**
Setup: a price quoted per portion, per piece, or per "box", billed per kilo. Big
in Istanbul spice markets, Marrakech, Southern European seafood restaurants.
Tell: the sign says a number and a tiny unit. Counter: confirm the unit out loud
and the total *before* they bag it or weigh it. Once it's cut, poured, or
cooked, the leverage is gone.

**"You touched it, you buy it."**
Setup: something is put in your hands or a sample is opened for you. Tell: it was
handed to you unasked. Counter: don't take anything into your hands. If it's
already there, set it down and step back — do not walk away holding it.

**The friend who isn't selling.**
Setup: someone practices English with you, walks with you, is not a salesman,
and eventually his cousin's shop happens to be right here. Tell: the entire
interaction has been unusually smooth. Counter: no obligation is created by
someone spending time on you. "My friend is waiting" and go.

## Food and drink

**No prices on the menu.**
Setup: tourist-strip restaurant, menu with no numbers, or a different menu for
foreigners. Tell: the absence of prices *is* the scam. Counter: don't sit down.
Ask for the printed price list before ordering; photograph the menu you ordered
from.

**Cover charges revealed at billing.**
Setup: per-person "service", "bread", "table". Note that some of these are legal
and normal — Italy's *coperto*, Spanish *cubierto*, Turkish table service —
when disclosed on the menu. Tell: undisclosed, or 15–25% "service" in a country
that doesn't do that. Counter: pay disclosed charges; contest undisclosed ones
by asking for the menu that shows them.

**Priced by weight, in small print.**
Setup: seafood or meat "at market price" or "€6" — per 100g. The bill is 10×
what you expected. Tell: any menu item priced without an obvious portion.
Counter: ask for the weight and the total in advance, in writing.

**The drinking-companion bill.** *(Istanbul, Shanghai/Beijing "tea ceremony" and
"art student", Tokyo *bottakuri* bars, Bucharest, Prague)*
Setup: a friendly local — often a pair, often a woman approaching a solo man —
suggests a bar they know. Drinks arrive. The bill is hundreds or thousands, and
staff position themselves near the door. Tell: **you did not choose the venue.**
Counter: this one is prevention-only. Never go to a bar chosen by someone who
approached you on the street. If already inside: this is a Rule 0 situation —
these places have muscle and card machines. Pay by card if you can (chargeable),
leave, dispute immediately, report to tourist police. Do not fight it in the room.

**Bill padding.**
Setup: items you didn't order, doubled quantities, arithmetic that doesn't add.
Tell: a total with no itemization. Counter: always ask for the itemized bill,
add it up in front of them, pay for what you ate. Errors are usually corrected
without argument the moment you show you're checking.

## Street and authority

**"It's closed today."**
Setup: near a major sight, a well-dressed man says it's shut — holiday, prayer,
restoration, a ceremony — and there's a better thing he can show you. Tell: no
other tourists are turning around. Counter: walk to the gate and look. This is
the single most common scam in Bangkok, Cairo, Delhi, and Istanbul, and the
entire structure collapses if you verify with your own eyes.

**Fake police.**
Setup: plainclothes "tourist police" or "narcotics" check your passport and your
wallet, often for "counterfeit currency", sometimes right after a stranger
offered you drugs or asked to change money. Tell: **real police do not need to
inspect your cash.** Counter: never hand over a wallet. Say you'll present
documents at the police station or to a uniformed officer, and start walking
toward somewhere public. Ask for ID and photograph it — genuine officers accept
this, fakes leave.

**The forced gift.** *(Rome and Paris bracelets, Athens and Istanbul rosemary,
Delhi flowers, pigeon feed at every European square)*
Setup: something is tied to your wrist, pressed into your hand, or your palm is
grabbed for a "free" reading — then payment is demanded and a scene is made.
Tell: physical contact from a stranger. Counter: hands in pockets, no eye
contact, keep walking. If it's already on your wrist: keep walking. Making a
scene is their tool, not yours.

**Distraction theft.**
Setup: sauce, mustard, or "bird droppings" on your jacket and a helpful cleaner;
a dropped wallet you're asked to identify; a clipboard petition pressed against
your chest; a fight or a fall right next to you. Tell: anything unexpected
touching you or crowding you. Counter: hands on your own pockets first, step
back, don't accept help, don't set a bag down to deal with it.

**ATM "help" and skimming.**
Setup: someone helpfully guides you through a machine, or an attachment sits over
the card slot. Tell: any interest in you at an ATM. Counter: use machines inside
bank branches during opening hours, cover the keypad, and refuse the ATM's
offer to convert to your home currency (see `anchors.md` on DCC).

**Fake tickets and fake queues.**
Setup: sellers outside a monument with a convincing line and "skip the line"
tickets. Tell: they're outside the official ticket area. Counter: buy at the
official window or the official site. A real skip-the-line reseller has a
storefront and a refundable booking confirmation.

**Cheap up, expensive down.** *(Giza camels and horses, Jaipur elephants,
Santorini donkeys, Petra)*
Setup: a low price to get on the animal. Once you're up, the descent, the return,
the photo, and the handler each cost extra. Tell: a price quoted for one
direction only. Counter: agree the round trip, the duration, and the photos as
one total, out loud, before mounting. Pay nothing until you're back on the
ground.

**The photo that isn't free.** Costumed characters, snake charmers, monkeys, and
anyone who poses. Ask the price first, or don't lift the camera.

## Accommodation and tours

**Bait-and-switch room.** The booked room is "flooded/overbooked", a worse or
pricier one is offered. Counter: booking confirmation in hand, insist on what you
paid for, and call the platform from the lobby — platform pressure works.

**The tour with no operator.** Deposit taken on the street or by a "travel
office" for a trip that doesn't exist or is resold badly. Counter: pay by card,
through a platform with a dispute window, never cash to a person on a street.

**"Tourist information" that's a travel agency.** Common in airport arrivals and
around major stations. Real tourist information doesn't sell you a transfer.

## Regional quick notes

Verify with a live search before relying on these — the specific scams rotate
faster than the categories do.

| Place | The ones that actually run |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Egypt** (Cairo, Giza, Luxor) | Mid-route renegotiation; camel up cheap / down expensive; papyrus and perfume factory stops; "the pyramids entrance is that way"; baksheesh demanded for unasked "help"; short-changing with the 50/200 EGP lookalikes |
| **Morocco** (Marrakech, Fes) | Unofficial guides who attach themselves in the medina, then charge; "the tannery viewpoint"; taxi meters "broken"; being walked deliberately deeper into the souk |
| **Turkey** (Istanbul) | The drinking-companion bill (solo men); shoeshine drops a brush; carpet shop via a new friend; spice market unit switch; "the Blue Mosque is closed" |
| **India** (Delhi, Agra, Jaipur) | Station ticket office "closed"; prepaid taxi desk imitations; tuk-tuk commission tours; hotel "burned down"; gem export deal |
| **Thailand** (Bangkok) | "Grand Palace closed today" → tuk-tuk gem tour; taxi meter refusal; jet-ski and scooter damage claims in Phuket/Samui |
| **Vietnam** (Hanoi, HCMC) | Taxi meters running fast, clone-livery cabs (check for Mai Linh/Vinasun spelling); dropped-zero confusion; shoeshine and doughnut-lady photo fees |
| **Indonesia** (Bali) | Scooter "damage" on return; unmetered airport taxis; ATM skimming; money-changer sleight-of-hand with the 10k/100k notes |
| **Italy / France / Spain** | Bracelet and rosemary gifts; restaurant bills without itemization near the sights; fake petitions; metro and Sagrada/Colosseum pickpocket teams; "skip the line" resellers |
| **Mexico / Colombia / Peru** | Unofficial airport taxis; ATM DCC and skimming; "the tour office is closed" reroutes; short-changing on large notes |
| **China** | Tea ceremony and "art student gallery" for solo travelers in Beijing/Shanghai tourist districts; unlicensed cabs outside stations |
| **Kenya / Tanzania** | Safari deposits to non-existent operators; airport transfer overcharges; "conservation fee" invented at gates |
