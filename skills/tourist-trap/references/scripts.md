# What to say

---

## Principles

- **Say a number, not a complaint.** "50." works. "That's way too expensive!"
  invites a conversation about your feelings, which you will lose.
- **Silence is the strongest move.** State your number, then stop talking. Most
  people fill silence by conceding. Let them.
- **Be bored, not angry.** Anger signals engagement, and engagement signals a
  deal is still available. Indifference ends it.
- **Never argue about fairness.** You cannot win "is this fair"; you can always
  win "this is what I will pay."
- **The walk-away is the only real leverage** — and it only exists if you can
  actually walk. Inside a car, a back room, or a bar with someone at the door,
  you don't have it. Don't play a hand you're not holding (Rule 0).
- **Name the alternative out loud.** "Bolt is 90" moves more than any adjective.
- **Carry small notes.** "I only have this" is unanswerable, and paying with a
  large note invites the short-change. Have the exact amount ready before you
  arrive.
- **Never pay before your things are out** of the trunk, the shop, the boat.

## Counter-offer ratios

Where haggling is expected, the first ask is an opening move, not a price.

| Setting | Counter at | Expect to land near |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| Egypt, Morocco, India street markets and taxis | 25–35% of the ask | 40–50% |
| Turkish bazaars, SE Asian night markets | 35–50% | 50–65% |
| Bali, Mexico markets, Balkan markets | 50–60% | 65–75% |
| Shops with tags in Europe, Japan, most of East Asia | don't haggle | the tag |

Rule: **only make an offer you'll actually pay.** If they accept instantly, you
opened too high — but you still pay it. Reneging on your own number is what
turns a negotiation into a fight.

Two phrases worth having in any language, because they're the ones that end
disputes:

- **"Itemized bill, please."** — *il conto dettagliato* (it) · *la cuenta
  detallada* (es) · *الفاتورة التفصيلية / al-fatura at-tafsiliya* (ar) ·
  *hesap dökümü* (tr) · 详细账单 (zh)
- **"Call the police."** — *chiamate la polizia* · *llame a la policía* ·
  *اتصل بالشرطة / ittasil bil-shurta* · *polisi arayın* · 报警

## Situational scripts

**Taxi stops mid-route and demands more.**
> Get your bag out of the trunk before you say anything about money. Then, from
> outside the car: "The price was 150. Here is 150." Exact change, in his hand,
> and start walking. Don't wait to be dismissed.

**Bill you didn't run up.**
> "Itemized bill, please." Photograph the menu. Add it up in front of them, out
> loud. Pay the items you ordered and put the cash on the table. If it escalates:
> "Fine — call the police." Legitimate restaurants call. The other kind doesn't.

**Plainclothes "police" wanting your wallet.**
> "I'll show my passport at the station." Don't open the wallet. Don't hand over
> anything. Photograph their ID, and move toward people while you do it.

**Something tied to your wrist / pushed into your hand.**
> Nothing. Keep walking. Do not stop, do not apologize, do not remove it while
> standing there. The scene is the leverage and it needs you to stay.

**A shop you're being kept in.**
> "My friend is waiting outside." Move to the door while saying it and keep
> moving. Don't finish the sentence standing still.

**A stranger leading you somewhere better.**
> "I'm meeting someone, thanks." Then go the other way — not the way they
> pointed, not even for a block.

**Pre-empting the taxi entirely.**
> Before the door closes: "How much to <destination>?" — get the number, repeat
> it back, and if there's a meter, "Meter, please." No number, no ride. This one
> sentence prevents most transport scams in this file.

## The escalation ladder

Climb one rung at a time. Most disputes end at rung 1 or 2.

1. **Repeat your number.** Same tone, no new arguments. Twice is enough.
2. **Name the alternative.** "Uber is 90." "The shop down the street has it at
   200." Facts beat volume.
3. **Add witnesses.** Move toward people. Hotel front desk, the neighboring shop,
   other tourists, staff. Scams need privacy; volume of onlookers is on your side.
   Turning a phone camera on is often enough — but read the room first, in some
   places filming provokes rather than de-escalates.
4. **Offer to call the police yourself.** Say it calmly and mean it. This is the
   sharpest rung: a real vendor shrugs, a scammer suddenly finds flexibility.
   In tourist economies the police are often genuinely on the visitor's side —
   the revenue depends on it.
5. **Pay and leave.** Then `recourse.md`.

**Skip straight to rung 5** whenever Rule 0 applies — isolated, outnumbered,
your things or documents held, anyone escalating physically. That isn't losing.
Card payments get reversed, app rides get refunded, and cash lost is cheaper
than the alternative.

## What not to do

- Don't grab your belongings back by force, or block a door.
- Don't threaten with police you won't call, or an embassy that won't come.
- Don't argue while inside a vehicle that's moving, or a room you can't exit.
- Don't let a dispute follow you to your hotel — never reveal which one it is
  mid-argument.
- Don't hand over a passport as a "deposit", ever — for a scooter, a room, a
  tour. Offer a photocopy or a cash deposit instead.
- Don't insist on winning. The goal is to leave with your things, at a price you
  can accept, not to be proven right.
